## A few hours is enough

To include everyone, an economy has to work for people who only have, or only want to give, a little time.

Most of the main economy expects full-time work: forty hours or more, on someone else's schedule. Many people can't do that, and many don't want to. So they're left out, or they wear themselves out trying.

We start from the other end. **There's no minimum.** An hour a week counts.

## Who this opens the door to

- **Students**, who bring energy and want to learn by doing real work
- **Retirees and older people**, who bring experience, patience and time to mentor
- **People with an illness or disability**, who can work in short stretches, from home, at their own pace
- **Parents and carers**, whose time comes in pieces
- **People with a full-time job** in the main economy, who'd like to build something on the side
- **Anyone in a busy season of life**

Each of them has something to offer, and an economy that can't use it is poorer for it.

## It's also how this gets built

For years, almost everyone who takes part will still meet most of their needs in the main economy. A movement that needed full-time commitment would only attract the few who can afford it. That's the opposite of what we want.

So small amounts of time aren't a compromise. They're the only way this grows.

## What it takes in practice

Making small amounts of time really count is a design problem, and we're working on it:

- **Small tasks.** Work cut into pieces someone can finish in one sitting.
- **Pairs and backups.** No team depends on one person always being available.
- **Working at different times.** Written notes and handovers, so people don't all need to be online together.
- **Short, honest commitments.** "I can give three hours a week for the next six weeks", and it's fine to stop or change.
- **Fair counting.** An hour from someone who has three spare hours a week should count the same as an hour from someone who has forty.

## Where it could lead

Over time, we'd like Our Economy to be a place where you can make a decent living on far fewer hours than today: say twenty a week, with the rest of your time for your interests, your family and your friends.

That's a direction, not a promise. Today, nobody is paid yet. But it's why we take time seriously from the start.

## Questions for you

- How much time could you realistically give, and when?
- What would make a small amount of time feel worthwhile to you, not wasted?
