## The core idea

In the main economy, a new company has to find customers among strangers, often competing with companies far bigger than itself. Most new companies fail at exactly that step.

We'd like to try a different starting point: **we make things for each other, and we buy from each other.**

If thousands of people each need food, repairs, childcare, websites, translation, bookkeeping, design and advice, then there's already a market among them. Members can serve it first, while they learn, and grow from there.

## Both sides of the deal

This only works if both halves happen:

- **Producing for each other.** Members and member companies offer their work inside the network.
- **Buying from each other.** Members and member companies choose to spend some of their money, or credit, inside the network too.

The second half is the hard part. It means changing some habits: choosing a member's service over a familiar brand, even when it's new and a little rough. We want to be honest that this is a real ask.

It starts small. A little of your time, a little of your spending. Nobody is asked to give up what works for them in the main economy.

## Starting with our own needs

The first customer is the movement itself. Even at the very beginning, Our Economy needs design, writing, translation, websites, legal research, bookkeeping, video and people to welcome newcomers. Each of these can be done by members, and later by member companies.

## Keeping track without much money

At first there may be little money to go around. Members can still trade with each other by keeping shared records: you did this for me, I did that for you. Some communities have done this for decades with time banks and community currencies.

How exactly we keep these records is something we'll design with the first group of people who join. The rule we'd start from: keep it simple, visible to everyone, and fair to people who give only a little time.

## Not a closed loop

The goal isn't to wall ourselves off. Member companies sell to the main economy too, and buy from it. Producing for each other is a safe place to start and to learn, and a steady base of customers when times are hard.

There's also a risk to watch: making things nobody really needs, just to keep the loop busy. Every piece of work should answer a real need.
