## Everyone

The short answer is: anyone who wants to take part. We start from everyone being in.

But we especially have in mind the many people the main economy doesn't serve well:

- **People who are left out.** Those who can't find steady work, or any work, wherever they live.
- **People who are badly served.** Those whose work doesn't pay enough to live on, or who can't get the services they need.
- **People who are worn out.** Those working too many hours, in too many jobs, to keep up.
- **People who are disillusioned.** Those who did everything they were told and still don't feel secure.
- **People who want something different.** Those doing fine, who'd still like a slower pace, more say in their work, or to help build something shared.

## The lucky few

In the main economy, some people make it. Some through skill and hard work, many through luck: where they were born, who they knew, what happened to be in demand that year.

But the main economy has only so many winning seats. However hard everyone tries, most people will never get one. That isn't a personal failing. It's how a competition works.

So most of us spend our lives competing with each other to be one of the lucky few. We'd like to try something else: working together to lift each other up.

## What everyone brings

Nobody arrives empty-handed. People bring:

- skills from work, from home, from life
- time, even a few hours a week
- local knowledge: what people near them need, who they know
- languages, which matter in a movement that's global from day one
- care: listening, welcoming, looking after others

With AI tools, it's easier than ever to learn something new and do useful work in an area you've never worked in before. Being "unqualified" matters less than it used to.

## Honest about today

We can't pay anyone yet, and we're at the very beginning. Today, "everyone's in" means there's real work for anyone who wants it, and a share in what we build together. We'll design how that share works with the first group of people who join.
