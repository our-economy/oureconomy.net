## One at a time is slow

Today, most new companies start one at a time. Someone with an idea, some savings and a lot of nerve tries to find customers among strangers and win against established competitors. Many fail. The ones who succeed are often people who already had money, connections, or luck.

If we relied only on that, and on the people who already hold capital deciding what gets built, most of the people we have in mind would never get to own anything.

## Companies that grow up together

We'd like to start companies **in groups**, chosen so they need each other:

- a design team that serves the web team
- a translation team that serves everyone
- a bookkeeping team that keeps the group's accounts
- a writing and video team that tells everyone's story

Each one starts as a **practice company**: real people doing real work for real needs, starting with the needs of the movement and of each other. What's "practice" at first is mainly the money and the paperwork. The work is real.

## This has been done before

Practice companies aren't new. Schools and training centres in over 40 countries run thousands of simulated companies that trade with each other through an international network (PEN Worldwide). Students learn by running a company: sending invoices, keeping accounts, dealing with customers and suppliers.

Our version goes one step further: the work and what it produces are real from the start, and the companies can grow up into real businesses.

## Graduating

A practice company "graduates" when it's ready to stand on its own. For example, when it lands its first customer outside the group, or covers part of its members' living costs.

Graduating doesn't mean leaving. Graduates keep serving the group, adopt a shared charter, and help start the next group of practice companies. That's how it snowballs: **each group of companies helps start the next one.**

## Why this could work faster

- **Customers from day one.** Each company already has others in the group who need its work.
- **Learning together.** Teams share tools, templates and mistakes, so nobody starts from zero.
- **Less risk on any one person.** Nobody bets their savings. If one company struggles, the group helps.
- **People can move.** Someone can try design, then bookkeeping, then sales, and find what suits them.

## What we don't know yet

- How to choose which companies start together
- How to keep internal trade honest, so nobody makes things just to keep busy
- When a company is ready to graduate
- How a graduated company shares what it earns with the group that helped start it

These are among the first experiments we want to run.

## Questions for you

- Which kind of practice company would you want to be part of?
- What would the first group need, to be useful to each other from the start?
