## Two reasons

There are two reasons to start now: the tools have arrived, and the window may not stay open.

## The tools have arrived

Things that used to need a big organization, lots of money, or years of training are now cheap or free:

- **Talking to anyone, anywhere.** Free video calls, chat and shared documents connect people across countries and time zones.
- **Computing power.** Running software, websites and shared records costs very little.
- **AI.** It helps people learn new skills fast, work in areas they were never trained for, and handle tasks like translation, writing, bookkeeping or code. It lets anyone be an expert in one thing and capable in many.
- **Free knowledge.** Encyclopedias, courses, books, open-source software and open designs, all free to use.
- **Know-how on working together.** A century of experience in running co-ops, plus tested models for teams, decisions and training, such as the practice-company networks used in schools around the world (see [Practice companies](/ideas/practice-companies/)).
- **Ways to keep commitments visible.** Digital agreements, shared records anyone can check, and community currencies that keep track of who gave what. They make it easier for strangers to trust each other.

A few decades ago, a global movement of people working a few hours a week, in many languages, building companies together would have been very hard to organize. Today it's possible.

## The window may not stay open

Building something new takes some slack: spare time, a little money, the energy to try something uncertain.

Many people already have very little slack. If more people end up working longer hours just to keep up, fewer will have the time and energy to help start something new. Starting while more people still have a little to spare is easier than starting later.

We don't know how things will go. But we'd rather build the lifeboat in good weather.

## Why not wait for others to do it?

Governments, companies and existing co-ops all have important roles. But none of them is set up to say "we take everyone" and mean it. Nobody needs permission to start, so we're starting.

## Questions for you

- Which of these tools do you already use? Which would you need help with?
- Do you see people around you with less spare time than a few years ago, or more?
