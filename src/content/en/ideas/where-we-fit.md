## A big, old family

Long before Our Economy, people were already building economies around working together and looking after each other:

- co-ops owned by the people who work in them, or by the people who use them
- credit unions and mutual insurance
- mutual aid groups and community kitchens
- time banks and community currencies
- community land trusts, gardens and energy projects
- open-source software and open knowledge, built by volunteers around the world

Around the world, many people call all of this the **solidarity economy**. Some call it the social economy, the cooperative movement, or something else again. Whatever the name, it's large, it's old, and it works. Millions of people rely on it every day.

**We see Our Economy as part of this family.** We're not inventing something new from nothing. We're building on more than a century of experience, and we hope to learn from everyone already doing this work.

## What we propose

Our contribution is a proposal for how this family could grow. In short:

- **Grow by design, not by accident.** Most co-ops stay small, and each one starts from scratch. We'd like every project and company to help start the next one, with people, tools and money.
- **Take everyone.** Not only those who can afford to join, or who already have the right skills. Everyone who wants a place gets one, with [any amount of time](/ideas/any-amount-of-time/).
- **Join up.** Groups share tools, a common fund, a talent pool and what they learn, so success in one carries over to the next. The Mondragon co-ops in Spain grew because they built shared institutions like these: their own bank, school and support services.
- **Start companies in groups.** New companies grow up together, using each other's work (see [Practice companies](/ideas/practice-companies/)).
- **Live alongside the main economy.** Not against it, and not instead of it. People and companies can [mix the two](/ideas/your-mix/) as they like.
- **Experiment in the open.** Try many ways of working, and share what we learn, including what didn't work.

## Why we don't lead with the name

On most of this site, we talk about "the main economy" and "Our Economy", not "the solidarity economy". That's not to keep a distance from the wider family. It's because the term means different things in different places, and many people have never heard it. We'd rather explain what we're doing in plain words that anyone, anywhere, can understand.

## Helping the family find each other

The family is large, but it's hard to see from the outside. Groups often don't know about each other, and newcomers don't know where to start. Strength in numbers only works if the numbers can find each other.

So one of the projects we work on is **[New Economy Hub](https://neweconomyhub.net)**: an open map of groups, events and online communities across this wider family, with a focus on ones a newcomer could join this week.

The hub isn't Our Economy, and it doesn't steer anyone toward us. It lists groups whatever their approach, because the whole family gets stronger when people can find each other. Our founder started it, and it's [open source](https://github.com/pinodeca/new-economy-hub), so you can help: suggest a group, an event or a forum, or improve the site.

It's early, too. So far it lists a few dozen groups, mostly in the United States. Each listing is researched with AI help and checked by a person before it goes up, and the site says so on every one.

## If you're already part of it

If you work in a co-op, run a community project, or have been building this for years: you know far more about some of this than we do. We'd love your advice, your criticism, and maybe your company. There'll be ways to link up at different levels, from simply sharing what we learn to working together closely.
