## A story

Imagine someone in their twenties who wants to try their luck in the main economy. They take a demanding job, or start a company, and go for it.

Ten years later, maybe they've succeeded. Maybe they haven't. Either way, life has changed: kids, a parent who needs care, or just a wish for a different pace. So they shift some of their time into Our Economy. They work on shared projects, help new teams get started, and make sure the people around them are okay.

Later, a new opportunity comes up in the main economy, and they shift back for a while. Nobody calls them a traitor or a quitter. That's how it's meant to work.

## Not a choice of sides

People can split three things between the two economies, in any mix:

- **Work:** where you spend your time
- **Spending:** who you buy from
- **Savings and investment:** where your money goes

And the mix can change at any time. All of these are fine:

- entirely in the main economy
- mostly main, with a little in Our Economy, as a hobby, a safety net, or a way to give back
- an even mix
- mostly in Our Economy
- entirely in it

Some people will always be better served by one or the other. That's fine too.

## Companies too

The same goes for companies. A company in the main economy can buy from member companies, take on people learning a trade, share profits with a common fund, or move gradually toward being owned by the people who work there. It can do as much or as little as suits it.

## What makes the choice real

For this freedom to mean something, a few things have to be true:

- **Coming back carries no penalty.** What you contributed stays recorded, and stays yours. The door stays open.
- **Success elsewhere is welcome.** Someone who did well in the main economy and comes back with money, skills or contacts is a gift, not a defector.
- **Failing elsewhere has somewhere to land.** If things go badly in the main economy, there's a place to go (see [Built for upheaval](/ideas/built-for-upheaval/)).

## Why this helps everyone

More options mean less desperation. Someone who knows they have somewhere to go makes better choices everywhere. They can leave a bad job, take a risk on a new idea, or take time to learn something new.

## Questions for you

- What mix would suit you right now? What about in ten years?
- What would make it easy to move between the two, in both directions?
