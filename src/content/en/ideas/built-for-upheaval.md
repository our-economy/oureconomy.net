## Designed to keep working

Economies have good years and bad years. Whole kinds of work appear and disappear. We think Our Economy can be designed to stay steady when the main economy shakes. That's one of the main reasons for building it.

This is a design goal, not a promise. Here's how we think it could work.

## When the main economy has a bad year

When money is short, people still have skills, time and needs. A network where members work and trade with each other, keeping their own records, can keep going when money dries up.

There's real evidence for this. In Switzerland, the WIR network has let businesses trade with each other using their own credit since 1934. The economist James Stodder studied it and found that its use tends to rise in recessions, exactly when ordinary money is scarce. It acts like a shock absorber for its members and, in a small way, for the country.

## When machines can do most of the work

AI and robots may one day do much of the work people do today. In the main economy, that could leave many people without an income.

In Our Economy, the people who do the work also own the companies, and everyone shares in a common fund. So when machines take over tasks, members own those machines. **Automation means less work for the same living, not no living.** That's the [twenty-hour week](/ideas/any-amount-of-time/) taken to its conclusion.

## Working more when it's needed, less when it isn't

Sometimes the world needs everyone to pitch in: after a disaster, during a pandemic, for a big shared project. Sometimes there's less to do.

We'd like to scale up and down fairly. When there's a real need, everyone puts in more hours. When there's less work, everyone works less, instead of some people losing everything. Germany's short-time work scheme (*Kurzarbeit*), where hours are cut across a whole workforce to avoid layoffs, is a well-known version of this idea in the main economy.

How to do this fairly is still an open question, and an experiment we want to run.

## A floor we build ourselves

Whatever governments decide about basic income or other public programs, we don't have to wait. Our Economy is security we build and own ourselves. It works with or without public programs, and it doesn't depend on anyone's goodwill.

## Good for the main economy too

A steady second economy helps the main one. People who have somewhere to land spend less time in crisis, recover faster, and can come back stronger.
