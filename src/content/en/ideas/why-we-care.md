## Both reasons count

People come to something like this for different reasons. Some want security: a place to work, to belong, to land when things go wrong. Some want to own part of what they build. Some want a slower pace and more time for the people they love.

Those are honest reasons, and they're welcome here. Nobody has to prove pure motives to join.

But there's another reason too. We care that other people do well. Not only our family, our friends or our town, but everyone, everywhere. And we believe that most of us will be more fulfilled living in an economy that's built to help everyone do well.

## Everyone, everywhere

Most of the world's great religions and ethical traditions share some form of the same idea: care for others as you care for yourself, and think of all of humanity, not just your own group.

We build on that common ground. We don't replace or compete with any religion or tradition. People of every faith, and of none, are equally at home here.

## Why say it out loud

A world where people care about each other is better. We think it's better still when that care is open and shared: when it's something we say to each other and build into how we work, not just a private hope.

It also matters in a practical way. We're asking strangers in different countries to commit to each other: to work together, share ownership, and help start the next team. That takes trust. A shared purpose, said out loud and practised every day, is how strangers come to trust each other.

## A belief we're testing

We don't claim this is proven. It's a belief, and Our Economy is partly an experiment in whether it holds up.

There are good signs. The Harvard Study of Adult Development has followed the same people, and later their children, since 1938. One of its clearest findings is that good relationships are among the strongest predictors of a long, healthy and happy life. More than money or fame.

An economy built around working together and looking after each other should be good at growing exactly those relationships. We want to find out.

## This isn't a judgement

Plenty of people in the main economy care deeply about others, and show it through their work every day. The main economy is built around competition, and this one is built around care. Neither has a monopoly on good people.
